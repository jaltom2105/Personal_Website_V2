Project Origin & Inspiration

This project was inspired by observing machine learning models learn complex tasks like playing soccer or "Red Light, Green Light" in virtual environments. Driven by a desire to understand how reinforcement learning functions from the ground up, I used Unity's Machine Learning Agents Toolkit (ML-Agents) to first master established baseline environments and then advance to designing, training, and benchmarking custom neural agents from scratch.

Project Goals

  • Understand RL in Practice: Master the mechanics of observations, policies, actions, and value estimation.
  • GPU-Accelerated Training on Modern Hardware: Configure an end-to-end pipeline running PyTorch with CUDA 12.8 acceleration on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 (Blackwell architecture).
  • Empirical Controlled Experiments: Test core hypotheses around neural network capacity, reward scaling ratios, and anti-reward hacking.
  • Custom Agent Design (Phase 4): Build and train a custom physics-driven ragdoll golfer learning to swing a club and hit a golf ball into a cup.
  • Rigorous Devlog Documentation: Maintain replicable logs, configurations, and TensorBoard telemetry.

Environments & Agents Explored

1. 3DBall — Dynamic Balancing Task

PERFECT SCORE (100.000)

12 agents training in parallel tilting platforms to keep a ball balanced on top. Demonstrates the core training loop with 8 continuous observations (ball position, ball velocity, platform rotation) and 2 continuous tilt actions.

Benchmark: Progressed from random failure (reward 1.206 at step 12k) to rapid improvement (49.964 at step 84k) to a perfect 100.000 score by step 120k (~2 minutes of RTX 5070 GPU training).

2. Crawler — Complex Multi-Joint Locomotion

HIGH-DIMENSIONAL CONTROL

A spider-like creature coordinating 20 continuous motor actions across 4 multi-segmented legs. Introduced key architectural innovations: local reference frames via an OrientationCube, raycasts measuring height, and joint-strength outputs.

Reward Gating: Employs multiplicative reward gating where matchSpeedReward × lookAtTargetReward ensures the agent only receives rewards when running fast AND facing the target simultaneously.

3. SoccerTwos — Multi-Agent Adversarial Competition

POCA ALGORITHM

Two teams of two competing in an automatically scaling difficulty curve. Uses the POCA (Multi-Agent Posthumous Credit Assignment) algorithm tracked via ELO ratings rather than static reward values.

Phase 3 Controlled Experiments

In Phase 3, I conducted three controlled experiments with isolated YAML configurations and C# agent code modifications to test reinforcement learning theory with empirical TensorBoard data:

EXPERIMENT 01 // ARCHITECTURAL CAPACITY

Network Size vs. Performance (64 vs. 128 vs. 256 Hidden Units)

Hypothesis: Larger neural networks learn faster due to greater representational capacity.
Result: All three networks (64, 128, and 256 hidden units) reached the perfect score of 100.000 at exactly step 132,000. Their TensorBoard reward, episode length, policy loss, and value loss curves were indistinguishable.
Finding: For low-dimensional tasks (8 observations, 2 actions), network capacity is not the bottleneck; even 64 units provides more than enough capacity.

EXPERIMENT 02 // REWARD DYNAMICS

Reward Scaling & Penalty Ratios (10:1 vs. 1:1)

Hypothesis: Reducing the ratio between penalty and survival reward destabilizes learning by weakening the failure signal.
Result: Changing survival reward from +0.1 to +1.0 (shifting penalty ratio from 10:1 down to 1:1) caused severe learning instability—reward climbed to 95.64 at step 120k, regressed down to 79.36 at step 132k, and only stabilized at step 156k.
Finding: The ratio between reward and penalty values is just as important as absolute magnitude. Strong failure signals prevent catastrophic policy oscillation.

EXPERIMENT 03 // BEHAVIORAL EXPLOITS

Reward Hacking (Removal of Penalty & Episode Resets)

Hypothesis: Removing penalties and resets causes agents to exploit unintended reward pathways.
Result: When failure was given identical +0.1 reward without resetting the episode, the agent reached a "perfect" 100 reward immediately by doing absolutely nothing. Balls dropped to the floor while platforms remained motionless.
Finding: "An agent does not learn what you want it to learn. It learns whatever maximizes the number you give it." Confirmed the vital need for multi-layered, unhackable reward shaping in custom agents.

Hardware & Engineering Solutions

Key Engineering Challenges Solved:

• RTX 5070 Blackwell (sm_120) GPU Acceleration: Stable PyTorch lacks sm_120 support; configured PyTorch nightly (2.12.0.dev) with CUDA 12.8 to unlock full GPU-accelerated training.

• Dependency Conflict Resolution: Handled ML-Agents installer attempts to downgrade PyTorch to CPU 2.8.0 by establishing automated reinstall and verification pipelines.

• Engine Upgrade: Upgraded from Unity 2022.3 LTS to Unity 6.4 (6000.4.3f1) with com.unity.ml-agents 4.0.3, eliminating import freezing and simulation stalling.
