HOW THE NEURAL DECISION BOUNDARY WARPS:

The Universal Approximation Theorem (Hornik, 1989): A standard feedforward neural network with a single non-linear hidden layer can approximate any continuous multidimensional function given sufficient neurons. When you train the network, backpropagation calculates partial derivatives of the cross-entropy loss relative to each weight ($\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w_{ij}}$) and applies gradient updates.

ReLU vs Tanh / Sigmoid:

Notice how ReLU produces piecewise-linear polygonal boundaries with sharp corners because its derivative is either 0 or 1. Conversely, Tanh and Sigmoid produce smooth, curvy manifolds due to continuous non-zero sigmoid gradients.

The XOR Problem & Depth:

Try the XOR preset: a single linear layer cannot separate the quadrants. But with 4+ hidden neurons or 2 layers, the network folds the 2D plane into a higher-dimensional space where the points become linearly separable.
