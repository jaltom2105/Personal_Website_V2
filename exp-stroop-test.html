THE STROOP EFFECT

Cognitive Interference & Reaction Time Laboratory

What is the Stroop Effect? First documented in 1935 by psychologist John Ridley Stroop, this experiment tests cognitive flexibility and selective attention.

When you read a word, your brain decodes its semantic meaning automatically and involuntarily. But when the visual ink color contradicts the written word (e.g. the word RED written in Blue ink), your brain experiences cognitive interference.

COGNITIVE SCIENCE & ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ANALYSIS:

Human Cognitive Processing: In human cognitive architecture, reading comprehension is an automated heuristic handled by the left ventral visual pathway. Suppressing semantic interference requires active top-down executive inhibition from the Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC) and Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC). Your measured interference cost of 0ms represents this neural conflict resolution latency.

Artificial Neural Networks: Traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) process pure numerical pixel tensors without semantic interference (0ms interference delay). However, modern Multimodal Foundation Models (like Vision Transformers trained on paired text-image tokens) have recently begun demonstrating artificial Stroop-like latency and attention bias when visual color tokens conflict with textual tokens in cross-attention matrices.
